## Findings and rationale

The [dated population findings](../research/menu-bar-load-cost.md#population-and-overview-feedback--2026-10-24) separate the user's screenshot, source inspection and a single aggregate process-table check. The app currently defaults to agent-associated scope with both resource filters off. Its badge counts unfiltered observed agent-associated lifetimes, including historical contributors in the displayed window, rather than busy workers or exact sessions. A large helper population is plausible; the check did not reconcile the exact `99+` badge or validate every attribution.

The present scalar history preserves overall resource trends, not a browsable archive of per-session workers. One-second accounting windows retain disappeared workers only within the delivered window; the next window can remove the evidence needed to explain a burst. This is a presentation/history gap distinct from work that acquisition never measured.

Recommendation: a default contributor overview plus bounded recent history, independent of acquisition cadence and disk recording. This reduces idle-process noise without sacrificing observed totals and makes bursts understandable after they finish. A process tree alone cannot solve ownership uncertainty, and a persistent recording alone cannot provide an always-available live overview when capture is off.

## Required contract

These are requirements of the proposed extension, not claims about the shipped app. Review findings B1–B4 and N1 are addressed by the disclosure, core observation, lifecycle, immutable-label compaction and explicit scope contracts below. The original acquisition, conservation and point-evidence limitations remain binding.

### Quantified disclosure and contributor selection

**Main contributors** is collapsed disclosure of the full population, not an automatically enabled resource filter. Every entry not individually expanded is represented in a remainder carrying its distinct process count, CPU over the current window, recent candidate CPU-seconds, current memory and missing/uncertain measurement counts. **Show all** expands that population. For example, `37 more processes · CPU 0.4% · Memory 210 MB · Recent candidate CPU 0.2 s` explains what has been collapsed. CPU/recent CPU/memory use their own stated populations; lifetime counts across historical session memberships are not additive.

Resource threshold filters remain off by default. When explicitly enabled, filtered observations still participate in totals and quantified remainder summaries, with a separate filtered count; disclosure does not silently disable a user's threshold choice. Acquisition, attribution and population totals never depend on how many rows are shown. An always-visible **Unresolved / uncertain** summary preserves work with missing or conflicting ownership; it has provider-qualified children where evidence supports them and does not invent a shared session. Separate shared-runtime summaries follow existing adapter recognition. These summaries and the remainder are not additional copies of the session totals.

Choose the following proposed initial rule consistently in Mac and TUI:

- Individually show the union of the top five exact session/root-session entries by CPU over the current window at or above **1% of one core**, and the top five by current footprint at or above **100 MB**. These are disclosure relevance floors, not accounting exclusions or enabled user thresholds.
- Keep recent activity discoverable when a session has at least **0.5 measured candidate CPU-seconds** or a **100 MB sampled worker footprint** within the retained recent period. Sessions that leave the individual top lists, including a formerly selected session during the next 60 seconds, remain in an expandable **Recent contributors** summary; do not grow an unbounded list of individually shown rows. Group the remaining exact sessions in the remainder. Each session appears in only one of these sections; pins appear once and remain inspectable.
- Preserve the displayed order of surviving individual entries. For a full ranking slot, a challenger replaces a still-eligible incumbent only above **120%** of that incumbent's corresponding metric; ties use provider-qualified session identity. Entries below a floor leave the individual ranking but remain accessible through recent/remainder disclosure. CPU and memory ranks are evaluated separately before taking the union. Unavailable values do not rank as zero; partial status remains visible in the owning summary.
- Around ten individually listed sessions is a default target, not a limit on accounting, pins, explicit expansion or access to additional contributors. No fixed top-N rule suppresses the quantified remainder. A zero-CPU memory-heavy session can qualify through memory; an entirely idle population is represented by its remainder and census rather than an empty claim of no processes.

These numerical rules are proposed usability defaults, not measured optima. Validate them with quiet-memory-heavy, burst-heavy and changing-rank cases. Session and executable groups initially collapse in Main contributors. This replaces the older, still-unapproved expanded-by-default suggestion in [PLAN FEEDBACK-01](../../PLAN.md#temporary-feedback-intake--2026-10-17) as the recommendation for this extension; explicit expansion and remembered disclosure continue to take precedence.

Remove the numeric census badge from the Mac status icon and keep the load bars. Put the qualified census in status text: `N observed agent-associated processes in this window, including M no longer observed`. The same census is available in the Mac/TUI overview. No replacement session-count badge is proposed: a small badge cannot explain candidate ownership, historical membership and coverage.

### Population hierarchy and executable grouping

Use corroborated root session → exact session/thread → executable basename → process. A single exact session needs no redundant root/thread row. Preserve thread identity below a multi-thread root instead of silently flattening different Codex threads into one executable population. Freeze each observation/contribution's root/thread corroboration result when core ingests it, and preserve that result in the closed unit. Root rollups deduplicate lifetimes and contributions; their frozen corroboration and candidate status remain inspectable. A current root runtime appearing or disappearing cannot regroup earlier work. Differing corroboration results within a unit remain separate compact categories. Project paths supply context, never ownership evidence.

Group by the case-preserving basename of the observed executable path: label `executable java`, `executable node`, etc. Paths that end in the same basename share this convenience group, even across installation/version directories, while full paths remain inspectable; this is not a claim that binaries or runtime implementations are identical. `node_repl` stays separate if it is the executable basename; a display name does not override the executable path. Missing executable evidence uses **Unknown executable**. Alias tables, version stripping and heuristics that map one executable to another are outside this delivery.

A contribution uses the executable observed at its closing observation, with the relevant observation identity retained. A detected path/name change across its endpoints is labeled **Executable changed across interval**; it does not prove when exec occurred, and unchanged paths cannot rule out same-path exec. Do not label all such intervals as verified “spans exec.” Later changes cannot rewrite earlier groups. A lifetime may appear in several historical executable/session categories, so parent distinct counts deduplicate rather than sum child counts. Preserve existing available JAR/script/tool descriptions; no unrestricted command/environment capture is added.

Observed ancestry is a separate relationship view, not session ownership. Preserve exact lifetimes, observation times and missing-parent/reparenting uncertainty; shared parents cannot imply an exact session. Initial Mac/TUI exploration uses existing expandable groups and details. A new native Explorer window and richer graphical ancestry navigation are deferred to separate approval; they are not required to deliver these statistics.

### Attribution unit and current/recent statistics

Aggregate session CPU **per unique contribution using its frozen session label**, both for the current window and the recent period. Do not reuse the lifetime-combined attribution in `AccountingWindow.findings` or sum its `uncertainCPUSeconds` to derive these session totals. Both frontends consume the same shared projection. No interval-supported session CPU is currently available, so label session CPU **point-evidence candidate CPU**, with unresolved/conflicting CPU separately visible. Process CPU still includes all of its measured contributions; a process total need not equal the subset attributed to a particular session.

Closed history has three session-resolution states: **unresolved** when no exact session is available, **point candidate** for one frozen provider/session identity, and **conflicting candidates** when evidence for that contribution actually conflicts. Provider/runtime-only evidence remains useful without an exact session. A missing label followed by a later resolved observation is not itself contradictory evidence and must not make the entire lifetime ambiguous; it also does not retrospectively assign earlier unlabeled CPU to the later session.

**No retroactive attribution revisions in this delivery.** Freeze each observation/contribution's available label when ingested, as the current pipeline does after resolving that scan; seal those individual labels when its unit closes. Never apply the last scan's label to the whole unit. Pending is only a transient display state for outstanding work in an open unit; any work still lacking an exact label at closure is retained as unresolved. Later provider listings, deferred evidence or ownership changes affect later observations/contributions only. The closed unit's label, executable, role and root-corroboration partitions are immutable, so no revision horizon or runner-up retention is needed.

This is an explicit scope reduction from the previous addendum, not a claim to implement the parent accounting contract's full historical-revision capability. That capability and authoritative recorded revisions remain separately gated with [diagnostic recording](diagnostic-recording.md). Reconsider revisions only if owned-workload evidence shows material short-worker CPU unresolved because evidence arrived late; any extension then needs a numeric revision horizon and a revised retention budget. Neither immutable labels nor recording may claim stronger ownership than the evidence supports.

Membership follows retained labeled observations/contributions. A worker can count once in each exact session for which it has a point label over the recent range; root/provider/global counts deduplicate its lifetime, and session counts are therefore not necessarily additive. A conflicting contribution appears once under uncertainty, with links to candidate sessions rather than duplicate CPU in each. Current memory uses the label of the final observation, independently of earlier CPU ownership. **No longer observed** always means absent from the final scan globally; remaining live under session B is a membership change, not a stopped worker in session A.

| Statistic | Public meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate CPU now | Unique point-labeled eligible CPU contributions divided by the latest enclosing display-window duration, in one-core percent. Show the actual duration whenever it exceeds the nominal report period, e.g. `CPU over 3.2 s`; do not imply an instantaneous reading. |
| Memory now | Latest available footprints of identified members observed in the final scan, under their current point labels. Qualify read spread, uncertainty and missing values; departed members supply no current memory. |
| Processes now | Distinct identified process lifetimes observed in the final scan, including adapter-recognised agent runtimes. This does not establish execution state or complete live-process coverage. |
| Recent workers | Distinct identified member lifetimes in the retained interval, excluding members recognised by an attribution adapter as agent runtimes at the relevant observation. Unclassified services remain workers; no generic Gradle/Kotlin/MCP service classifier is assumed. Runtime/workload role changes are disclosed and parent lifetime counts deduplicate them. |
| No longer observed | Recent identified worker lifetimes globally absent from the final scan. Returns and expiry can reduce this count; it is neither monotonic nor confirmed termination. |
| Recent candidate CPU-seconds | Unique accepted CPU contributions with point session labels and endpoints eligible in the actual recent interval, including departed workers. Keep unresolved/conflicting CPU and older-opening recovery separate. Never prorate a crossing interval or add displayed percentages. |
| Largest sampled worker footprint | Maximum retained acquisition reading for the worker and stated executable/session category in the interval, with its observation time and point evidence. It is not its last reading, a sum over time, or the true unobserved peak. |
| Last observed kernel task peak | Last retained successful `ri_lifetime_max_phys_footprint` reading with its observation time and point evidence, even after disappearance. It may reset on exec and may describe a peak before monitoring or before the recent period. Peak time and exec epoch are unverified; it is not a recent-window maximum. |
| Coverage | Actual retained range and segment, missing observations, recovery exclusions, unresolved/conflicting attribution and retention loss. Listed-but-unreadable PIDs without identity are observation counts, never distinct-worker counts. |

For example, `Candidate CPU now 0% · Memory now 1.6 GB · 8 processes now (6 workers, 2 agent runtimes)` can coexist with `Recent: 24 workers no longer observed · 6.3 candidate CPU-seconds`. Values are illustrative. Details expose executable groups and individual workers with exact identity, first/last observation, CPU, sampled maxima and kernel-peak observations. “Recent” does not assert a verified short lifetime: first appearance is not birth, last observation is not exit, and the observed span is not a proven lifetime. Historical rows remain ineligible for process actions.

### Core ownership, process scope and compact records

Core owns acquisition-time memory aggregation and the per-run recent history store. The store follows the collector's configured process scope: **agent-associated by default**, **all same-user processes** with TUI `--all-processes`, with the collector's existing explicit exclusions. At unit closure, retain all categories for an identified lifetime if any of its frozen observations/contributions in that unit qualifies for the configured scope; this preserves mixed known/unresolved work within an included lifetime without attributing its unresolved portion to a session. A later unit's label does not retroactively admit an earlier out-of-scope unit. Thresholds and Main contributors never narrow this store. The latest all-user/host CPU context continues to use its wider accounting population independently of history scope.

A scope change resets recent history and labels its new start; do not splice unlike populations into one recent range. Mac and TUI share results for the same scope, observations and retained interval. All-process mode can reach the same capacity limits sooner and therefore report a shorter interval; that explicit coverage difference is not a disagreement in accounting.

Global final-scan presence for retained identities is checked against the collector's full identified scan, even when a lifetime's new label no longer qualifies for history scope. Leaving agent scope is not disappearance. Current memory and ownership come from that latest observation, never from an older compact record just because its lifetime is still present. A prior candidate session can retain recent CPU without retaining that worker's current memory under the old owner.

Compact each closed pre-coalescing core window into one record per **exact lifetime × frozen semantic session label/resolution state × observed executable × adapter runtime/worker role × frozen root/thread corroboration**. Session labels include provider/runtime identity and candidate sets needed to preserve uncertainty. Changing observation timestamps or repeating equivalent point labels does not create a new key. Missing measurements do not erase identified membership. Each compact record contains:

| Field | Retained meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Within-unit CPU | Sum of unique accepted CPU-seconds whose opening scan is at or after this unit's baseline and whose closing scan is in the unit. Preserve valid-zero versus no accepted measurement and missing-read/exclusion summaries. |
| Sampled footprint maximum | Largest footprint in this category within the unit, with the winning observation ID/time and its frozen point evidence. Equal maxima use earliest observation order. |
| Latest footprint observation | Latest value or unavailable state, observation ID/time and label. It supplies current memory only when that observation belongs to the final scan; an older value remains historical even if the lifetime is still present. |
| Last successful kernel peak | Latest successful kernel task peak with observation ID/time/label, even when lower than the prior successful reading; separately record later unavailability. |
| Membership and point evidence | First/last observation, runtime/worker role, frozen root/thread result, bounded semantic evidence summary and its observed-time range/missing-time count. Maxima/latest readings retain their own supporting evidence. This is summarized point evidence, not a replayable archive of every original observation. |

Aggregate memory before latest-only rows overwrite intermediate readings. A 100 MB peak in category A and an 80 MB peak in category B remain separate. With immutable category labels and whole-unit eviction, a range maximum is exactly the maximum of the retained per-unit maxima: an older maximum's runner-up is either another retained unit's maximum or belongs to the same unit being removed. Raw within-unit footprint readings need not remain after sealing. A kernel peak is not a recent-window maximum, cannot be maxed across possible exec boundaries, and must not be labeled “since last exec” without verified epoch evidence. Optional session memory peaks require additional per-scan aggregates; they are not derivable from compact worker maxima.

Keep an individual record, including original ID and opening/closing endpoints, for **each crossing CPU contribution whose opening predates its own unit baseline**. Do not also include that CPU in the compact within-unit sum. Preserve its frozen category so wider-window recovery can be assigned without consulting the current lifetime row. This is exact for the declared whole-unit range queries; it does not support arbitrary cuts inside a compacted unit or reconstruct original per-scan rates.

### Wire and recording boundary

Keep compact history and memory fields in a typed in-memory core payload separate from existing Codable report shapes. **JSONL v6 remains unchanged**: no new recent fields are implicitly encoded through `HeavyProcess` or `ReportWindowSummary`, and existing values keep their meanings. The shared interactive session projection consumes immutable contribution-category totals rather than legacy lifetime-combined attribution. Exposing compact statistics in machine output requires an explicit later schema version.

Live history works with recording off. The [recording proposal](diagnostic-recording.md) remains authoritative for persistent raw observations, attribution revisions, gaps and counter replay. Compact history is a derived live summary, not an alternative authoritative recording format or a replacement for raw records. Recording-backed Explorer and longer-term analysis remain outside this delivery. Neither feature recovers never-observed workers or final exit CPU.

### Retention units, conservation and capacity

Target **60 seconds** of recent activity, retaining complete compact core windows **before presentation coalescing**. A unit is identified by acquisition run and baseline/final scan; units partition closing scans. Core admits each canonical unit once, including an accepted partial unit on failure, independently of panels and slow consumers. Deduplicate contribution IDs before compaction. While a unit's validation context is retained, an identical duplicate is a no-op and a conflicting copy is an explicit history error. A bounded run/sequence admission watermark ignores already-evicted older deliveries as stale without claiming their contents match; no fingerprint archive is retained indefinitely. An incoming range overlapping an admitted range and extending past that watermark is rejected rather than partially added. Delivery carries a bounded immutable projection, not a backlog of raw scans.

Whole units make within-unit sums safe: every opening admitted to such a sum is at or after that unit's baseline, and every closing belongs to that unit. Any retained range made from whole consecutive units therefore includes those endpoints; adding their sums counts the CPU once without recovering or prorating it. Crossing contributions are evaluated separately against the retained range baseline/final scans: an opening before the range stays separately recovered, while an opening within the range becomes eligible exactly once. Wall/monotonic enclosing intervals can overlap by scan duration, so use enclosing endpoints for rates; do not add window durations or percentages.

Evict oldest complete units by time or capacity, keeping a contiguous suffix in a retention segment. Do not discard selected compact categories or crossing contributions inside an otherwise retained unit. For the time target, keep units whose baseline is inside the target period. If the newest unit alone exceeds 60 seconds, retain it only when it fits the capacity bounds and show its actual duration; do not split or prorate it. Show shortened coverage such as `Recent 37 s (retention limit)`. A boundary observation is context, not a newly measured worker or peak. A history gap starts a new segment; its boundary excludes earlier-opening CPU even if collection continued.

Proposed limits remain **65,536 logical records** and **16 MiB retained UTF-8 text**, independent of the pipeline's limits. Count compact records, individual crossing contributions, unit metadata and separately retained identity/evidence/ancestry context; variable-size context elements count individually. Fixed inline numeric/time/identity fields belong to their containing record, rather than being counted again as raw observations. Share equivalent immutable context where possible, but count actual retained text and copies. This budget covers core history, host-owned published/pending projections and one frozen inspection projection. Open-unit work remains subject to the existing pipeline bounds and must not be duplicated into an unbounded raw-history queue.

| Illustrative stable population at 100 ms acquisition and one-second units | Former raw footprint + contribution records per minute | Compact category records per minute |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 90 agent-associated lifetimes, one category each | 108,000 | 5,400 |
| 613 same-user lifetimes, one category each | 735,600 | 36,780 |

These are arithmetic examples, not a measured retained duration: they exclude metadata/context, label/role changes, crossing contributions, churn, extra held projections and text costs. Compaction removes the mandatory scan-rate multiplier for stable within-unit work; it does not guarantee that every workload fits 60 seconds. Establish actual record/text/heap costs in acceptance measurements before claiming typical retention. Raw-observation retention for future revisions would need a different numeric budget or horizon.

Evict oldest unpinned units before accepting another. If a unit cannot fit, report a history gap and unavailable/shortened statistics while live accounting continues. Frozen backing data counts against the same limits; do not silently mutate it to make space. Record/text caps are engineering bounds, not a measured Swift heap limit; allocation and copy-on-write costs remain acceptance work.

### Lifecycle, pause and shared presentation

Key history by acquisition run, with segment boundaries for retention gaps. The host retains the core history service across collector replacement; it is not owned solely by the short-lived collection task or resettable inspection state. Survive native `resetCollection()` on both panel open and close; those operations reset inspection, not recent measurements. In live-icon mode, closed-panel windows continue to feed core history, and reopening exposes the latest retained projection without requesting a new collector or losing the burst that prompted inspection. Both Mac and TUI use the same accounting/statistics service; shared presentation owns grouping, disclosure and selection over its immutable results.

On acquisition-run replacement, discard the prior run's recent history and any frozen projection, start fresh baselines, and visibly label the reset. Do not retain a previous-run browser or combine CPU across run IDs. A pause survives retention eviction within its run, not an explicit run replacement. Static-icon mode stops collection on close and labels the new run **Collected since panel opened** on reopening. Live-icon close/open retains the same run and history. App/TUI exit discards in-memory history. Acquisition's monotonic clock governs CPU intervals; retention age and clock/sleep gaps remain explicit rather than being treated as sampled activity. Within one run, retention-gap segments may remain inspectable within the budget, but current/recent totals use only the latest contiguous segment.

Pause freezes a self-contained, bounded projection including session statistics, worker/executable drill-down and required evidence, not references that ordinary eviction can invalidate. Keep at most one such inspection projection per host; all its backing data counts against the history bound. If it prevents retaining later units, preserve inspection and show the resulting live-history gap on resume rather than silently deleting frozen detail. Closing and reopening clears pause/inspection under existing native behavior; history survives only if the acquisition run remains the same. Unpause shows the latest available projection and actual retained coverage.

## Proposed decisions and approval boundary

The revised proposal selects quantified disclosure, the explicit ranking/floor/stability rule, executable-basename grouping, a core-owned pre-coalescing history store scoped to the collector, immutable closed-unit labels and exact per-unit compaction with individual crossing contributions, the 60-second target and record/text bounds, unchanged JSONL v6, run-aware lifecycle, per-contribution point-candidate statistics, removal of the numeric badge and deferral of the Explorer window. These replace the first draft's open design alternatives; they still require proposal approval and validation, not another implicit interpretation by an implementer.

The canonical instruction clarification permits quantified disclosure while keeping resource threshold filters off and totals independent. It does not activate the proposed feature. [PLAN](../../PLAN.md#resource-accounting-priority) records this review stage and reconciles the earlier expanded-group idea. Exact UI layout, measured heap cost and suitability of the proposed ranking constants remain acceptance work, not grounds to weaken the accounting contract.

## Acceptance semantics and material risks

Mac and TUI must produce identical current/recent session and executable statistics for identical core observations, scope and range. Individually shown, recent, unresolved/shared and remainder sections must conserve CPU/memory within their stated populations and deduplicate distinct counts. Thresholds/disclosure cannot reduce totals. Exercise root/thread membership, repeated/coalesced deliveries, PID reuse, later resolution affecting only later observations, changed executable labels and a live worker changing session without disappearing.

Retain a memory spike at acquisition scan 3 even when scan 10 is lower and delivery later coalesces windows. Compare compact sums/maxima with an independent raw-observation fixture for every retained whole-unit suffix, including changing labels/executables/roles, root runtime departure, valid zero, unavailable readings, crossing intervals and duplicate-unit delivery. Frozen root corroboration cannot be replaced by today's graph. A 100 MB and 80 MB observation in different frozen categories retain both maxima; unresolved readings cannot later migrate to a session. Preserve last kernel-peak evidence without assigning it to the recent interval or current footprint. Unknown-then-resolved observations keep separate unresolved/candidate CPU, while actual conflicts remain uncertain. Missing-identity PID reads affect Coverage only.

Exercise live-icon close/open, static-mode run replacement, failure partial windows, history overflow, rolling-window expiry, crossing recovered intervals and frozen-detail eviction pressure. Run/scope replacement discards previous history; neither a new run nor a retention gap becomes continuous CPU. Verify that agent-only and all-process stores apply their declared populations before retention, independently of filters, and compare Mac/TUI projections over identical retained scope/ranges. Missing observations, shortened/oversized recent ranges and incomplete history remain visible. Ranking must keep quiet-memory-heavy and historical burst contributors discoverable without exploding the compact list or overriding inspection.

Actual heap cost, ranking usability and native/TUI layouts remain unvalidated. The process population check is not an attribution oracle. Complete churn/exit capture and interval-supported historical ownership remain the accounting proposal's existing open gates; a useful overview does not close them.
