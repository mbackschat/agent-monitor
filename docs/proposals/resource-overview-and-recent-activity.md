# Resource overview and recent activity

Status: **unapproved**, 2026-10-24. Addendum to [resource accounting](resource-accounting.md), informed by the user's 2026-10-24 feedback and discussion. The user accepted the Main contributors direction and requested this consolidated design; the revised contract and defaults below remain proposed. The first external review is assessed in the [review findings](../research/menu-bar-load-cost.md#overview-addendum-review--2026-10-24); the separate Explorer window is deferred from this delivery. This document does not extend the original first-delivery implementation authorization or approve [diagnostic recording](diagnostic-recording.md). Open work belongs in [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md#resource-accounting-priority).

## Findings and rationale

The [dated population findings](../research/menu-bar-load-cost.md#population-and-overview-feedback--2026-10-24) separate the user's screenshot, source inspection and a single aggregate process-table check. The app currently defaults to agent-associated scope with both resource filters off. Its badge counts unfiltered observed agent-associated lifetimes, including historical contributors in the displayed window, rather than busy workers or exact sessions. A large helper population is plausible; the check did not reconcile the exact `99+` badge or validate every attribution.

The present scalar history preserves overall resource trends, not a browsable archive of per-session workers. One-second accounting windows retain disappeared workers only within the delivered window; the next window can remove the evidence needed to explain a burst. This is a presentation/history gap distinct from work that acquisition never measured.

Recommendation: a default contributor overview plus bounded recent history, independent of acquisition cadence and disk recording. This reduces idle-process noise without sacrificing observed totals and makes bursts understandable after they finish. A process tree alone cannot solve ownership uncertainty, and a persistent recording alone cannot provide an always-available live overview when capture is off.

## Required contract

These are requirements of the proposed extension, not claims about the shipped app. Review findings B1–B4 are addressed by the disclosure, core observation, lifecycle and attribution contracts below. The original acquisition, conservation and point-evidence limitations remain binding.

### Quantified disclosure and contributor selection

**Main contributors** is collapsed disclosure of the full population, not an automatically enabled resource filter. Every entry not individually expanded is represented in a remainder carrying its distinct process count, CPU over the current window, recent candidate CPU-seconds, current memory and missing/uncertain measurement counts. **Show all** expands that population. For example, `37 more processes · CPU 0.4% · Memory 210 MB · Recent candidate CPU 0.2 s` explains what has been collapsed. CPU/recent CPU/memory use their own stated populations; lifetime counts across historical session memberships are not additive.

Resource threshold filters remain off by default. When explicitly enabled, filtered observations still participate in totals and quantified remainder summaries, with a separate filtered count; disclosure does not silently disable a user's threshold choice. Acquisition, attribution and population totals never depend on how many rows are shown. An always-visible **Unresolved / uncertain** summary preserves work with missing or conflicting ownership; it has provider-qualified children where evidence supports them and does not invent a shared session. Separate shared-runtime summaries follow existing adapter recognition. These summaries and the remainder are not additional copies of the session totals.

Choose the following proposed initial rule consistently in Mac and TUI:

- Individually show the union of the top five exact session/root-session entries by CPU over the current window at or above **1% of one core**, and the top five by current footprint at or above **100 MB**. These are disclosure relevance floors, not accounting exclusions or enabled user thresholds.
- Keep recent activity discoverable when a session has at least **0.5 measured candidate CPU-seconds** or a **100 MB sampled worker footprint** within the retained recent period. Sessions that leave the individual top lists, including a formerly selected session during the next 60 seconds, remain in an expandable **Recent contributors** summary; do not grow an unbounded list of individually shown rows. Group the remaining exact sessions in the remainder. Each session appears in only one of these sections; pins appear once and remain inspectable.
- Preserve the displayed order of surviving individual entries. For a full ranking slot, a challenger replaces a still-eligible incumbent only above **120%** of that incumbent's corresponding metric; ties use provider-qualified session identity. Entries below a floor leave the individual ranking but remain accessible through recent/remainder disclosure. CPU and memory ranks are evaluated separately before taking the union. Unavailable values do not rank as zero; partial status remains visible in the owning summary.
- Around ten individually listed sessions is a default target, not a limit on accounting, pins, explicit expansion or access to additional contributors. No fixed top-N rule suppresses the quantified remainder. A zero-CPU memory-heavy session can qualify through memory; an entirely idle population is represented by its remainder and census rather than an empty claim of no processes.

These numerical rules are proposed usability defaults, not measured optima. Validate them with quiet-memory-heavy, burst-heavy and changing-rank cases. Session and executable groups initially collapse in Main contributors. This replaces the older, still-unapproved expanded-by-default suggestion in [PLAN FEEDBACK-01](../../PLAN.md#temporary-feedback-intake--2026-10-17) as the recommendation for this extension; explicit expansion and remembered disclosure continue to take precedence.

Remove the numeric census badge from the Mac status icon and keep the load bars. Put the qualified census in status text: `N observed agent-associated processes in this window, including M no longer observed`. The same census is available in the Mac/TUI overview. No replacement session-count badge is proposed: a small badge cannot explain candidate ownership, historical membership and coverage.

### Population hierarchy and executable grouping

Use corroborated root session → exact session/thread → executable basename → process. A single exact session needs no redundant root/thread row. Preserve thread identity below a multi-thread root instead of silently flattening different Codex threads into one executable population. Root rollups deduplicate lifetimes and contributions; their observed corroboration and candidate status remain inspectable. Project paths supply context, never ownership evidence.

Group by the case-preserving basename of the observed executable path: label `executable java`, `executable node`, etc. Paths that end in the same basename share this convenience group, even across installation/version directories, while full paths remain inspectable; this is not a claim that binaries or runtime implementations are identical. `node_repl` stays separate if it is the executable basename; a display name does not override the executable path. Missing executable evidence uses **Unknown executable**. Alias tables, version stripping and heuristics that map one executable to another are outside this delivery.

A contribution uses the executable observed at its closing observation, with the relevant observation identity retained. A detected path/name change across its endpoints is labeled **Executable changed across interval**; it does not prove when exec occurred, and unchanged paths cannot rule out same-path exec. Do not label all such intervals as verified “spans exec.” Later changes cannot rewrite earlier groups. A lifetime may appear in several historical executable/session categories, so parent distinct counts deduplicate rather than sum child counts. Preserve existing available JAR/script/tool descriptions; no unrestricted command/environment capture is added.

Observed ancestry is a separate relationship view, not session ownership. Preserve exact lifetimes, observation times and missing-parent/reparenting uncertainty; shared parents cannot imply an exact session. Initial Mac/TUI exploration uses existing expandable groups and details. A new native Explorer window and richer graphical ancestry navigation are deferred to separate approval; they are not required to deliver these statistics.

### Attribution unit and current/recent statistics

Aggregate session CPU **per unique contribution using its frozen session label**, both for the current window and the recent period. Do not reuse the lifetime-combined attribution in `AccountingWindow.findings` or sum its `uncertainCPUSeconds` to derive these session totals. Both frontends consume the same shared projection. No interval-supported session CPU is currently available, so label session CPU **point-evidence candidate CPU**, with unresolved/conflicting CPU separately visible. Process CPU still includes all of its measured contributions; a process total need not equal the subset attributed to a particular session.

Distinguish four session-resolution states on retained observations/contributions: **pending** when relevant resolution work is actually outstanding; **unresolved** when no exact session is available; **point candidate** for one frozen provider/session identity; and **conflicting candidates** when evidence for that contribution actually conflicts. Provider/runtime-only evidence can remain useful while exact-session resolution is pending or unresolved. A missing label followed by a later resolved observation is not itself contradictory evidence and must not make the entire lifetime ambiguous. It also does not retrospectively assign earlier unlabeled CPU to the later session.

The core extension must represent pending resolution explicitly rather than infer it from `.unknown` or `.runtimeOnly`. A late label for an earlier contribution requires evidence referring to that original observation, with original evidence time and an explicit replacement revision; a newer registry observation alone is insufficient. Apply an accepted revision once, preserve prior provenance and move rather than duplicate the affected CPU. Pending records become unresolved when resolution ends without a label, the acquisition run ends, or their retained context expires. Bounded revision/context retention and visible loss are part of the history budget. This extends the existing revision-1-only implementation; it does not claim that pending/revision handling is already shipped.

Membership follows retained labeled observations/contributions. A worker can count once in each exact session for which it has a point label over the recent range; root/provider/global counts deduplicate its lifetime, and session counts are therefore not necessarily additive. A conflicting contribution appears once under uncertainty, with links to candidate sessions rather than duplicate CPU in each. Current memory uses the label of the final observation, independently of earlier CPU ownership. **No longer observed** always means absent from the final scan globally; remaining live under session B is a membership change, not a stopped worker in session A.

| Statistic | Public meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Candidate CPU now | Unique point-labeled eligible CPU contributions divided by the latest enclosing display-window duration, in one-core percent. Show the actual duration whenever it exceeds the nominal report period, e.g. `CPU over 3.2 s`; do not imply an instantaneous reading. |
| Memory now | Latest available footprints of identified members observed in the final scan, under their current point labels. Qualify read spread, uncertainty and missing values; departed members supply no current memory. |
| Processes now | Distinct identified process lifetimes observed in the final scan, including adapter-recognised agent runtimes. This does not establish execution state or complete live-process coverage. |
| Recent workers | Distinct identified member lifetimes in the retained interval, excluding members recognised by an attribution adapter as agent runtimes at the relevant observation. Unclassified services remain workers; no generic Gradle/Kotlin/MCP service classifier is assumed. Runtime/workload role changes are disclosed and parent lifetime counts deduplicate them. |
| No longer observed | Recent identified worker lifetimes globally absent from the final scan. Returns and expiry can reduce this count; it is neither monotonic nor confirmed termination. |
| Recent candidate CPU-seconds | Unique accepted CPU contributions with point session labels and endpoints eligible in the actual recent interval, including departed workers. Keep unresolved/conflicting CPU and older-opening recovery separate. Never prorate a crossing interval or add displayed percentages. |
| Largest sampled worker footprint | Maximum retained acquisition reading for the worker and stated executable/session category in the interval, with its observation time and point evidence. It is not its last reading, a sum over time, or the true unobserved peak. |
| Last observed kernel task peak | Last retained successful `ri_lifetime_max_phys_footprint` reading with its observation time and point evidence, even after disappearance. It may reset on exec and may describe a peak before monitoring or before the recent period. Peak time and exec epoch are unverified; it is not a recent-window maximum. |
| Coverage | Actual retained range and segment, missing observations, recovery exclusions, pending/unresolved/conflicting attribution and retention loss. Listed-but-unreadable PIDs without identity are observation counts, never distinct-worker counts. |

For example, `Candidate CPU now 0% · Memory now 1.6 GB · 8 processes now (6 workers, 2 agent runtimes)` can coexist with `Recent: 24 workers no longer observed · 6.3 candidate CPU-seconds`. Values are illustrative. Details expose executable groups and individual workers with exact identity, first/last observation, CPU, sampled maxima and kernel-peak observations. “Recent” does not assert a verified short lifetime: first appearance is not birth, last observation is not exit, and the observed span is not a proven lifetime. Historical rows remain ineligible for process actions.

### Core observation ownership and wire boundary

Core owns acquisition-time memory retention and the per-run recent accounting store. Preserve identified membership observations per executable/session/worker-role category within each unit even when CPU or footprint is unavailable; counts cannot depend on successful resource reads. Before any observation is replaced by the latest per-lifetime row, retain its sampled footprint maximum, original observation ID/time and frozen point attribution; retain separate maxima for differing executable/session labels as well as the overall worker maximum. A higher maximum from session B must not erase session A's smaller historical maximum. Window merges take maxima of compatible categories, retain the winning observation, deduplicate IDs and resolve equal-value ties by observation order. Presentation cannot reconstruct these values from delivered latest-only rows.

A maximum alone is insufficient while attribution can revise. Retain the original footprint observations and their identity/time/label context throughout each retained unit's revision horizon, within the same history budget, so a valid revision can recompute both the source and destination category maxima. For example, pending readings of 100 MB and 80 MB cannot be reduced irreversibly to 100 MB if the 80 MB reading may later acquire a distinct session label. Capacity eviction removes the whole unit and its maxima together. Any other loss of required revision context makes the affected maximum explicitly incomplete/unavailable; never preserve a stale exact maximum or replace a lost runner-up with zero.

Retain the latest successful kernel-peak observation separately, replacing it with later successful readings even when lower. A failed later read does not make the older successful value current; show its retained timestamp and later unavailability. Do not max kernel peaks across possible exec boundaries or label the result “since last exec” without verified epoch evidence. Optional session memory peaks require additional per-scan aggregates and are not derivable by summing worker maxima.

Keep these history/memory/resolution extensions in a typed in-memory core payload separate from the existing Codable report shapes. **JSONL v6 remains unchanged**: its existing fields keep their semantics; new recent statistics, pending states and retained memory fields are not implicitly encoded through `HeavyProcess` or `ReportWindowSummary`. The shared interactive session projection consumes per-contribution labels rather than legacy lifetime-combined rows. Extending machine output to expose this new contract requires an explicit later schema version; do not silently add properties or change the meaning of existing v6 values.

### Retention unit and accounting boundaries

Target a rolling **60 seconds** of recent activity, using complete core accounting windows **before presentation coalescing** as retention units. Identify each unit by acquisition run and baseline/final scan. Core history ingestion occurs once at window closure, including accepted partial windows on failure, independently of open panels or slow consumers. This avoids making the retained range depend on how a frontend combines its delivered reports. Delivery carries a bounded immutable projection of retained history, not an unbounded backlog.

Evict oldest complete units by time or capacity, preserving a contiguous suffix within each retained segment. Do not drop individual contribution or memory-category records from a retained unit. For the time target, retain whole units whose baseline is inside the target period; if the newest unit alone is longer than 60 seconds, retain it only if it fits the capacity bound and label its actual longer duration. Never split or prorate it to manufacture exactly 60 seconds. After startup or eviction, show the actual interval, e.g. `Recent 37 s (retention limit)`. A boundary observation is context only, not a newly measured worker or memory peak.

Whole-unit eviction does **not** eliminate crossing contributions. A CPU interval closing in a retained unit can open in an evicted unit after a read gap. Recompute eligibility using the enclosing retained baseline/final scans: earlier-opening CPU remains separately recovered with original endpoints and is excluded from the range's rate. Widening a valid same-run range can restore eligibility once. Scan-index windows partition closing observations; their wall/monotonic enclosing intervals can overlap by scan duration, so neither durations nor percentages are additive. Duplicate/revised deliveries cannot duplicate accepted contributions.

Proposed history-wide bounds are **65,536 retained logical records** and **16 MiB of retained UTF-8 text**, independent of the pipeline's own bounds. Account for contribution, identity, original memory observations/category summaries, evidence/revision and ancestry records; count text and retained references, not just visible rows. The budget includes retained old segments, backing data for host-owned published/pending projections and one frozen inspection projection. Shared immutable storage is counted once; extra retained copies/context also count. Evict whole oldest unpinned units before accepting another. If one unit cannot fit, expose a history gap and unavailable/shortened statistics while live accounting continues; never claim a complete recent range. A gap starts a separate retention segment and cannot be crossed by CPU baselines. These are engineering caps, not a measured Swift heap bound; retained allocations and copy-on-write costs remain an acceptance measurement.

### Lifecycle, pause and shared presentation

Key history by acquisition run, with segment boundaries for retention gaps. The host retains the core history service across collector replacement; it is not owned solely by the short-lived collection task or resettable inspection state. Survive native `resetCollection()` on both panel open and close; those operations reset inspection, not recent measurements. In live-icon mode, closed-panel windows continue to feed core history, and reopening exposes the latest retained projection without requesting a new collector or losing the burst that prompted inspection. Both Mac and TUI use the same accounting/statistics service; shared presentation owns grouping, disclosure and selection over its immutable results.

A new acquisition run starts a visibly labeled new segment, with fresh baselines and no CPU crossing run IDs. Retain older segments only within the same history budget for separate inspection; never merge their current/recent totals with the new run. Static-icon mode collects only while the panel is open and labels the new live segment **Collected since panel opened**. Earlier segments are labeled previous collection and may expire while collection is stopped; new views cannot make old data appear recent. App/TUI exit discards in-memory history. Acquisition's monotonic clock governs within-run CPU; collection timestamps and an independent retention clock govern history age across stops, with clock/sleep gaps disclosed rather than treated as sampled activity.

Pause freezes a self-contained, bounded projection including session statistics, worker/executable drill-down and required evidence, not references that ordinary eviction can invalidate. Keep at most one such inspection projection per host; all its backing data counts against the history bound. If it prevents retaining later units, preserve inspection and show the resulting live-history gap on resume rather than silently deleting frozen detail. Closing and reopening clears pause/inspection under existing native behavior but preserves the collected history. Unpause shows the latest available projection and actual retained coverage.

### Recording relationship

Live recent history works with recording off. The [recording proposal](diagnostic-recording.md) remains authoritative for persistent raw observations, attribution revisions, gaps and counter replay. Core summaries are not a second authoritative recording format and must not replace raw counter records. A later recording-backed Explorer must expose capture boundaries and gaps; both that window and longer-term analysis are outside this delivery. Neither live history nor recording recovers never-observed workers or final exit CPU.

## Optional and excluded scope

| Capability | Boundary and rationale |
| --- | --- |
| Separate native Explorer / graphical ancestry navigation | Deferred to separate approval. Existing Mac/TUI disclosure and details are sufficient for the initial shared statistics and avoid making a new window a prerequisite. |
| Peak observed session footprint | Optional, still gated: requires retained per-scan aggregates and attribution evidence referring to those observations. Resolution is asynchronous, so labels can be pending or arrive later; today's labels cannot reconstruct prior membership. Any point-attributed aggregate must state its evidence strength and scan spread. The original stronger historical-peak requirements remain unchanged. |
| Monotonic total observed since collection started | Optional and not in this delivery. Exact distinct counts need bounded identity-retention guarantees and explicit start/reset boundaries; never silently reset or count returning identities again while claiming monotonic distinct totals. |
| Configurable recent range / recording-backed analysis | Deferred beyond the proposed 60-second default. No automatic disk recording or cross-run CPU continuity is implied. |
| Confirmed stopped counter | Excluded without reliable exit evidence. A disappearance-event counter counts events, not distinct stopped workers, and is not the recommended overview statistic. |
| Classification and completeness guesses | Excluded: cwd-based ownership, executable aliases inferred from display names, PID-only identity, summing duplicate views or individual memory peaks, treating unreadable workers as idle, and hiding gaps for a cleaner display. |
| Other product expansion | No new process actions, live provider probes, automatic uploads/exports, persistent capture by default, acquisition adaptation or wire-schema changes are included. |

## Proposed decisions and approval boundary

The revised proposal selects quantified disclosure, the explicit ranking/floor/stability rule, executable-basename grouping, a core-owned pre-coalescing history store, the 60-second target and record/text bounds, unchanged JSONL v6, run-aware lifecycle, per-contribution point-candidate statistics, removal of the numeric badge and deferral of the Explorer window. These replace the first draft's open design alternatives; they still require proposal approval and validation, not another implicit interpretation by an implementer.

The canonical instruction clarification permits quantified disclosure while keeping resource threshold filters off and totals independent. It does not activate the proposed feature. [PLAN](../../PLAN.md#resource-accounting-priority) records this review stage and reconciles the earlier expanded-group idea. Exact UI layout, measured heap cost and suitability of the proposed ranking constants remain acceptance work, not grounds to weaken the accounting contract.

## Acceptance semantics and material risks

Mac and TUI must produce identical current/recent session and executable statistics for identical core observations, scope and range. Individually shown, recent, unresolved/shared and remainder sections must conserve CPU/memory within their stated populations and deduplicate distinct counts. Thresholds/disclosure cannot reduce totals. Exercise root/thread membership, repeated/coalesced deliveries, PID reuse, late resolution/revisions, changed executable labels and a live worker changing session without disappearing.

Retain a memory spike at acquisition scan 3 even when the last observation at scan 10 is lower and a later delivered window is coalesced. A valid revision that splits earlier 100 MB/80 MB pending readings between sessions must recover both maxima from retained observations; removing a category's winner must expose its valid runner-up or an explicit context-loss state. Preserve departed workers' last kernel-peak evidence without assigning it to the recent interval or current footprint. Unknown-then-resolved contributions retain their separate unresolved/candidate CPU; genuinely conflicting evidence remains uncertain. Missing-identity PID reads affect Coverage only. Late-arriving labels cannot retroactively supply interval support or erase prior evidence.

Exercise live-icon close/open, static-mode run replacement, failure partial windows, history overflow, rolling-window expiry, crossing recovered intervals and frozen-detail eviction pressure. A new run or retention gap never becomes continuous CPU. Missing observations, shortened/oversized recent ranges and incomplete history remain visible. Ranking must keep quiet-memory-heavy and historical burst contributors discoverable without exploding the compact list or overriding inspection.

Actual heap cost, ranking usability and native/TUI layouts remain unvalidated. The process population check is not an attribution oracle. Complete churn/exit capture and interval-supported historical ownership remain the accounting proposal's existing open gates; a useful overview does not close them.
